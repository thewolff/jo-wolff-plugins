## 1. Title links (TTL-)

Credentials: none. Cross-page checks (TTL-004) need a crawl or sitemap sample.

| rule-id | check | layer | severity + why | source URL | quoted passage |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| TTL-001 | Page has a non-empty `<title>` element in `<head>` (check raw HTML and rendered DOM). | raw-html, rendered | high: title is a primary input to the title link; missing title forces Google to guess from other sources | https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/title-link | "Make sure every page on your site has a title specified in the `<title>` element." |
| TTL-002 | Title is descriptive, not a vague label such as "Home" or "Profile". | raw-html | low: advisory, only partly machine-checkable (flag exact matches of a short stop-list) | https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/title-link | "Avoid vague descriptors like "Home" for your home page, or "Profile" for a specific person's profile." |
| TTL-003 | Title does not repeat the same words or phrases (keyword stuffing); flag comma-separated keyword lists. | raw-html | medium: stuffing "can make your results look spammy" | https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/title-link | "It's sometimes helpful to have a few descriptive terms in the `<title>` element, but there's no reason to have the same words or phrases appear multiple times." |
| TTL-004 | Titles are distinct across pages (compare sampled pages of the site; flag identical or boilerplate-only titles). | raw-html | medium: identical titles make results indistinguishable | https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/title-link | "It's important to have distinct text that describes the content of the page in the `<title>` element for each page on your site." |
| TTL-005 | Title is not half-empty (starts or ends with a bare separator such as "\| Site Name" or "Site Name -"). | raw-html | low: Google substitutes headings, but the signal is degraded | https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/title-link | "Half-empty `<title>` elements: When part of the title text is missing." |
| TTL-006 | Title uses the same language and writing system as the page's primary content (compare script of title vs. body text). | raw-html | low: Google may substitute another text | https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/title-link | "If Google determines that a `<title>` element does not match the writing system or language of the page's primary content, we may choose a different text as the title link." |
| TTL-007 | The page has one clearly most-prominent main title (first visible `<h1>` or largest heading) and it is consistent with the `<title>`. | rendered | low: only affects which text Google picks | https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/title-link | "If Google Search detects that there are multiple large, prominent headings, it may use the first heading as the text for the title link." |
| TTL-008 | `<title>` is not obsolete relative to the visible heading (for example a different year or season number). | rendered | low: Google corrects it in the title link | https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/title-link | "Google Search may detect this inconsistency and uses the right date from the visible title on the page in the title link" |
| TTL-009 | Page is crawlable if the title must come from page content: a robots.txt-blocked page gets a title link built from off-page signals such as anchor text. | robots, headers | medium: ties TTL to RBT/IDX | https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/title-link | "If we don't have access to the content on your page, we will rely on off-page content to generate the title link, such as anchor text from other sites." |
| TTL-010 | Title brand placement: site name at start or end with a delimiter, not a long tagline repeated on every page. | raw-html | low: advisory | https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/title-link | "consider including just your site name at the beginning or end of each `<title>` element, separated from the rest of the text with a delimiter such as a hyphen, colon, or pipe" |

## 3. Canonicalization and duplicate URLs (CAN-)

Credentials: none for page-level checks. Google's chosen canonical (as opposed to the declared one) is visible only in Search Console URL Inspection; without it the audit can only check declared signals for consistency.

| rule-id | check | layer | severity + why | source URL | quoted passage |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| CAN-001 | If `<link rel="canonical">` is present, it is inside `<head>` (not in `<body>`), and `<head>` contains no invalid elements before it. | raw-html, rendered | high: out-of-head canonical is not accepted | https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/consolidate-duplicate-urls | "The `rel="canonical"` `link element` is only accepted if it appears in the `<head>` section of the HTML" |
| CAN-002 | Canonical href is an absolute URL (scheme + host), not relative. | raw-html | medium: relative works but is fragile (for example a crawled test site) | https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/consolidate-duplicate-urls | "Use absolute paths rather than relative paths with the `rel="canonical"` `link` element." |
| CAN-003 | The canonical page carries a self-referential canonical. | raw-html | low: listed as a "Do" | https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/consolidate-duplicate-urls | "include a `rel="canonical"` `link` on the canonical page itself (also known as a self-referential canonical)." |
| CAN-004 | Canonical signals agree: HTML canonical, `Link: rel="canonical"` header, sitemap `<loc>`, redirect target and hreflang self-URL all name the same URL. | raw-html, headers, sitemap | high: contradictory signals; also "more error prone" when both HTML and header are used | https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/consolidate-duplicate-urls | "Don't specify different URLs as canonical for the same page using different canonicalization techniques (for example, don't specify one URL in a sitemap, but a different URL for that same page using `rel="canonical"`)." |
| CAN-005 | Do not declare both HTML element and HTTP header canonical on the same URL unless the values are identical. | raw-html, headers | medium: Google recommends choosing one | https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/consolidate-duplicate-urls | "We recommend that you choose one of these and go with that; while supported, using both methods at the same time is more error prone" |
| CAN-006 | Canonical URL has no `#fragment`. | raw-html | medium: fragments are generally unsupported | https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/consolidate-duplicate-urls | "Don't specify a URL fragment as canonical, as Google generally doesn't support URL fragments." |
| CAN-007 | Raw-HTML canonical equals rendered-DOM canonical (JavaScript must not change it). If only JS can set it, it must be absent from raw HTML. | raw-html, rendered | high: conflicting canonical across render states | https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/consolidate-duplicate-urls | "The best way to do this is to specify the canonical URL in the HTML source code and make sure that JavaScript doesn't change the canonical link element." |
| CAN-008 | Canonical `<link>` has no `hreflang`, `lang`, `media` or `type` attribute (those annotations are ignored for canonicalization). | raw-html | low: attribute silently ignored | https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/consolidate-duplicate-urls | "`rel="canonical"` annotations with `hreflang`, `lang`, `media`, and `type` attributes are not used for canonicalization." |
| CAN-009 | HTTPS page does not undermine its own canonical status: valid certificate for the host, no insecure (non-image) subresources, no redirect to or through HTTP, no canonical to the HTTP URL, no HTTP URLs in sitemap or hreflang. | headers, rendered, sitemap | high: Google "prefers HTTP very strongly" in these cases and HSTS cannot override it | https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/consolidate-duplicate-urls | "Avoid bad TLS/SSL certificates and HTTPS-to-HTTP redirects because they cause Google to prefer HTTP very strongly." |
| CAN-010 | For non-HTML documents (PDF etc.) an HTTP `Link: <url>; rel="canonical"` header with an absolute URL is the only canonical mechanism; note it applies to web search only. | headers | low: informational | https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/consolidate-duplicate-urls | "Google supports this method for web search results only." |
| CAN-011 | Internal links on the page point to the canonical URL form (host, protocol, trailing slash, case), not to duplicate URLs. | rendered | medium: consolidates signals | https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/consolidate-duplicate-urls | "When linking within your site, link to the canonical URL rather than a duplicate URL." |
| CAN-012 | robots.txt is not used as the duplicate-handling mechanism (disallowed URLs can still be indexed without content). | robots | medium | https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/consolidate-duplicate-urls | "Don't use the robots.txt file for canonicalization purposes. Google may still index URLs that are disallowed in robots.txt without their content." |
| CAN-013 | `noindex` is not used to pick a canonical within a site. | raw-html, headers | medium: it blocks the page entirely | https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/consolidate-duplicate-urls | "We don't recommend using `noindex` to prevent selection of a canonical page within a single site, because it will completely block the page from Search." |
| CAN-014 | `<head>` contains only valid elements (`title, meta, link, script, style, base, noscript, template`); `iframe` or `img` in `<head>` ends Google's reading of the head, so canonical, robots, hreflang and meta placed after it are ignored. | raw-html | high: silently drops later metadata | https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/valid-page-metadata | "If you use an invalid element in the `<head>` element, Google ignores any elements that appear after the invalid element." |
| CAN-015 | URL case consistency: if the server treats upper and lower case identically, URLs should be normalised to one case. | raw-html, headers | low | https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/url-structure | "If upper and lower case text in a URL is treated the same by your web server, convert all text to the same case so it's easier for Google to determine that URLs reference the same page." |
| CAN-016 | With hreflang, the canonical is a page in the same language (or best substitute). | raw-html | medium | https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/consolidate-duplicate-urls | "make sure to specify a canonical page in the same language, or the best possible substitute language if a canonical page doesn't exist for the same language." |
| CAN-017 | Redirect-based canonicalization uses server-side permanent redirects (301/308) for deprecated duplicates (host, protocol, `/home`). | headers | medium | https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/consolidate-duplicate-urls | "For the quickest effect, use HTTP (also known as server-side) redirects." |

## 5. robots.txt: Google interpretation and RFC 9309 (RBT-)

Credentials: none. Search Console robots.txt report not needed; audit fetches `/robots.txt` for the page's exact scheme, host and port.

| rule-id | check | layer | severity + why | source URL | quoted passage |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| RBT-001 | `/robots.txt` is reachable at the top level of the same scheme, host and port as the audited URL. Subdirectory robots.txt files are not valid. A missing file (4xx) is not an error. | robots, headers | low (info) | https://developers.google.com/crawling/docs/robots-txt/robots-txt-spec | "You must place the robots.txt file in the top-level directory of a site, on a supported protocol." |
| RBT-002 | robots.txt does not return 5xx, 429 or fail with network or DNS errors. Google stops crawling the site for the first 12 hours, may use a cached copy for up to 30 days, and stops crawling if the site stays unavailable. | robots, headers | critical: whole-site crawl stoppage | https://developers.google.com/crawling/docs/robots-txt/robots-txt-spec | "For the first 12 hours, Google stops crawling the site but keeps trying to fetch the robots.txt file." |
| RBT-002b | RFC 9309 treats unreachable robots.txt as complete disallow (Google differs after cache expiry: no robots.txt). | robots | | https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9309 | "If the robots.txt file is unreachable due to server or network errors, this means the robots.txt file is undefined and the crawler MUST assume complete disallow." |
| RBT-003 | 4xx (except 429) on robots.txt means "no restrictions": report it as info, and warn if a 403/401 wall likely hides an intended disallow. 429 is treated as a server error. | robots, headers | low | https://developers.google.com/crawling/docs/robots-txt/robots-txt-spec | "Google's crawlers treat all `4xx` errors, except `429`, as if a valid robots.txt file didn't exist." |
| RBT-003b | 429 handling on the generic status doc. | headers | | https://developers.google.com/crawling/docs/troubleshooting/http-status-codes | "Google's crawlers treat the `429` status code as a signal that the server is overloaded, and it's considered a server error." |
| RBT-004 | robots.txt redirects resolve within five hops; more than five hops are treated as a 404 (no rules). JavaScript, meta-refresh and frame redirects are not followed. | robots, headers | medium | https://developers.google.com/crawling/docs/robots-txt/robots-txt-spec | "Google follows at least five redirect hops as defined by RFC 1945 and then stops and treats it as a `404` for the robots.txt file." |
| RBT-004b | RFC wording. | robots | | https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9309 | "If there are more than five consecutive redirects, crawlers MAY assume that the robots.txt file is unavailable." |
| RBT-005 | robots.txt size is at most 500 KiB; content beyond the limit is ignored. | robots | high if exceeded: later rules silently dropped | https://developers.google.com/crawling/docs/robots-txt/robots-txt-spec | "Google enforces a robots.txt file size limit of 500 kibibytes (KiB). Content which is after the maximum file size is ignored." |
| RBT-005b | RFC minimum parsing limit. | robots | | https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9309 | "The parsing limit MUST be at least 500 kibibytes [KiB]." |
| RBT-006 | robots.txt is UTF-8 plain text (RFC: Internet Media Type `text/plain`); lines separated by CR, CR/LF or LF. A BOM is ignored. If HTML is served instead, Google extracts rule-like lines and ignores the rest. | robots, headers | medium | https://developers.google.com/crawling/docs/robots-txt/robots-txt-spec | "The robots.txt file must be a UTF-8 encoded plain text file and the lines must be separated by `CR`, `CR/LF`, or `LF`." |
| RBT-006b | RFC content type requirement. | headers | | https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9309 | "The file MUST be UTF-8 encoded (as defined in [RFC3629]) and Internet Media Type "text/plain" (as defined in [RFC2046])." |
| RBT-007 | Only `user-agent`, `allow`, `disallow`, `sitemap` are used by Google. `crawl-delay` and other fields are ignored. Report them as ineffective. | robots | low | https://developers.google.com/crawling/docs/robots-txt/robots-txt-spec | "Google supports the following fields (other fields such as `crawl-delay` aren't supported):" |
| RBT-008 | Rule paths start with `/` (or are empty); paths are case-sensitive; rules with no path are ignored. Flag `Disallow: admin` style paths and case mismatches against real URLs. | robots | medium | https://developers.google.com/crawling/docs/robots-txt/robots-txt-spec | "The path value must start with `/` to designate the root and the value is case-sensitive." |
| RBT-009 | Rules appear inside a group (after a `user-agent` line); rules before the first `user-agent` are ignored (RFC: SHOULD ignore). | robots | medium | https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9309 | "The crawler SHOULD ignore "disallow" and "allow" rules that are not in any group" |
| RBT-010 | Group selection: only one group applies to a crawler (the most specific matching user agent); a specific group is NOT combined with the `*` group. Check that `User-agent: Googlebot` groups carry every restriction intended for Googlebot. | robots | high: a specific group silently overrides `*` rules | https://developers.google.com/crawling/docs/robots-txt/robots-txt-spec | "Only one group is valid for a particular crawler." "User agent specific groups and global groups (`*`) are not combined." |
| RBT-011 | `Sitemap:` lines use fully qualified URLs; they are not bound to a user agent and do not terminate a group. | robots | low | https://developers.google.com/crawling/docs/robots-txt/robots-txt-spec | "It must be a fully qualified URL, including the protocol and host, and doesn't have to be URL-encoded." |
| RBT-011b | Sitemap line is global, but still subject to being crawlable. | robots | | https://developers.google.com/crawling/docs/robots-txt/robots-txt-spec | "The sitemap field isn't tied to any specific user agent and may be followed by all crawlers, provided it isn't disallowed for crawling." |
| RBT-011c | RFC: other records must not terminate a group. | robots | | https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9309 | "Parsing of other records MUST NOT interfere with the parsing of explicitly defined records in Section 2. For example, a "Sitemaps" record MUST NOT terminate a group." |
| RBT-012 | Wildcards: `*` matches 0 or more characters; `$` anchors the end; `/` and `/*` block everything below root. Evaluate whether the audited URL (including query string) is allowed for Googlebot and Googlebot-Image using these semantics. | robots | critical when the audited URL or its CSS/JS is blocked | https://developers.google.com/crawling/docs/robots-txt/robots-txt-spec | "`*` designates 0 or more instances of any valid character." "`$` designates the end of the URL." |
